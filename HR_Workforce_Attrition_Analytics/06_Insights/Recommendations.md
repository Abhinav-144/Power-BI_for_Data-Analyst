# HR Recommendations

Recommendations are based on patterns visible in the current report. They are suggestions for investigation, not claims that a particular factor caused attrition.

## Priority 1 — Investigate high-attrition roles
Sales Representatives show the highest displayed job-role attrition rate (39.76%).
- Review exit interview themes.
- Examine sales targets, incentive structure, and workload.
- Compare turnover patterns across teams and tenure groups.

## Priority 2 — Examine overtime patterns
The displayed attrition rate is 30.53% for employees working overtime versus 10.44% for those not working overtime.
- Check overtime frequency and distribution.
- Review staffing capacity and scheduling.
- Gather employee feedback before changing policies.

## Priority 3 — Strengthen early-career support
The 18–25 age group has the highest displayed age-group attrition rate (35.77%).
- Review onboarding effectiveness.
- Offer mentoring and role-specific training.
- Clarify career paths and performance expectations.

## Priority 4 — Improve employee experience
Employees with job satisfaction rating 1 show a 22.84% attrition rate, compared with 11.33% for rating 4.
- Review employee feedback and exit reasons.
- Investigate recognition, manager support, and development opportunities.
- Track whether changes are followed by improvements in later measurements, if dated data becomes available.

## Priority 5 — Review work-life balance
28.84% of employees have work-life balance ratings of 1 or 2.
- Explore workload, schedule flexibility, and overtime.
- Compare patterns across job roles and departments.
- Avoid assuming that all employees with lower ratings have the same concerns.

## Priority 6 — Validate before acting
- Check the number of employees in each subgroup; small groups can produce volatile rates.
- Compare rates as well as counts.
- Confirm that slicers are cleared when reporting company-wide results.
- Validate findings with qualitative feedback and additional data.
- Avoid causal language when the analysis is observational.
