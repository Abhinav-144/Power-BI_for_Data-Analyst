# Business Insights — HR Workforce & Attrition Analytics

These findings are transcribed from the visible Power BI dashboard screenshots. Recheck them against the final report before publication.

## Overall workforce
- Total employees: **1,470**
- Exited employees: **237**
- Overall attrition rate: **16.12%**
- Average tenure of exited employees shown on the Attrition Analysis page: **5.13 years**

## 1. Department attrition
- Sales: **20.63%**
- Human Resources: **19.05%**
- Research & Development: **13.84%**

**Finding:** Sales has the highest displayed department attrition rate.

**Recommendation:** Investigate workload, management practices, compensation, career opportunities, and exit feedback in departments with higher attrition.

## 2. Job-role attrition
- Sales Representative: **39.76%**
- Laboratory Technician: **23.94%**
- Human Resources: **23.08%**
- Sales Executive: **17.48%**
- Research Scientist: **16.10%**
- Manufacturing Director: **6.90%**
- Healthcare Representative: **6.87%**
- Manager: **4.90%**
- Research Director: **2.50%**

**Finding:** Sales Representatives have the highest displayed job-role attrition rate.

**Recommendation:** Review role-specific workload, incentives, career progression, and employee exit feedback.

## 3. Age-group attrition
- 18–25: **35.77%**
- 26–30: **21.29%**
- 31–35: **17.49%**
- 51+: **12.45%**
- 41–45: **9.38%**
- 36–40: **9.06%**

**Finding:** The 18–25 group has the highest displayed attrition rate. Note: verify age-group labels and sorting against the final chart before publication.

**Recommendation:** Review onboarding, mentoring, training, and early-career development.

## 4. Overtime and attrition
- Overtime = Yes: **30.53%**
- Overtime = No: **10.44%**

**Finding:** Employees working overtime have a higher displayed attrition rate.

**Recommendation:** Review overtime frequency, staffing, workload, and employee feedback. This is an association, not proof that overtime causes attrition.

## 5. Job satisfaction and attrition
The matrix shows:
- Satisfaction 1: 289 employees, 66 exits, **22.84%** attrition
- Satisfaction 2: 280 employees, 46 exits, **16.43%** attrition
- Satisfaction 3: 442 employees, 73 exits, **16.52%** attrition
- Satisfaction 4: 459 employees, 52 exits, **11.33%** attrition

**Finding:** The lowest job-satisfaction rating has the highest displayed attrition rate.

**Recommendation:** Review employee feedback, recognition, management support, and career development opportunities.

## 6. Work-life balance
The donut chart shows:
- Rating 1: 80 employees, **5.44%**
- Rating 2: 344 employees, **23.40%**
- Rating 3: 893 employees, **60.75%**
- Rating 4: 153 employees, **10.41%**

**Finding:** Most employees have a work-life balance rating of 3; 28.84% have a rating of 1 or 2.

**Recommendation:** Examine workload, scheduling flexibility, and employee experience among those reporting lower ratings.

## 7. Job satisfaction by department
- Sales: **2.75**
- Research & Development: **2.73**
- Human Resources: **2.60**

**Finding:** Human Resources has the lowest displayed average job satisfaction of the three departments, while the differences are modest.

**Recommendation:** Explore employee feedback and related measures before deciding on interventions.

## 8. Environment satisfaction
The dashboard displays average environment satisfaction of approximately **2.7** across the three departments.

**Finding:** Department-level averages are similar at the displayed precision.

**Recommendation:** Explore results by job role, job level, and overtime status to look for more specific patterns.

## Interpretation note
These findings describe patterns in the dataset. They do not establish causal relationships. Check figures, labels, filters, and measure definitions in the final PBIX before external publication.
